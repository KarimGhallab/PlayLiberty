Screens

Library

The route a user lands on, and the only entry point to content. It is a header and a list: no sidebar, no tab bar, no persistent chrome of any kind.

Two arrangements, and not one arrangement and a variant. Web is / and mobile is the app root. Every measurement below says which one it came from, and the two differ in the header's height, in the name column's width, in the surface, and in what the user can reach at all. A reader who reads only the web half can tell it is half.

The web arrangement, at 1056

1056 is the content column this file's own page frame produces at its max width, and it is not the 1014 the empty-state record quotes: that is the same column one step in, inside a panel, and a screen is not a panel. The header is 78.4px, a --text-2xl title and a 36px control on one line.

Each item is the playlist header, at the header's own 136px. Not a row that resembles one. The first version of this screen drew an 88.8px row carrying the identity block alone at 64px and --text-md, and it had its own cover, its own name line, its own count and its own share indicator under five class names of its own. It also reproduced flex: 1 on the name, which the header's own comment calls the mistake that line made first, and it looked right only because a count lane happened to bound it. Five new class names are invisible to anything that compares file names, so nothing caught it. The component's rules now live once in specimen.css and both specimens use them.

The item measures 136px and every item after the first 137px, the extra pixel being the header's own border-top between two of them. The item is a door and not a play: it carries the shuffle and the overflow and nothing else, so its actions column is the action row alone at 72px and the name keeps its full 848px. The play, the transport and the equalizer strip belong to a playlist that is opened and played, and are drawn on the detail rather than on a gallery of destinations.

The name truncates on web now, which it did not when the item was 88.8px and the name was 16px. At --text-xl the last row's 78-character name is 923px against an 820px column and is cut. The item before it, 32 characters, is 356px and is not.

The mobile arrangement, at 390

390 wide, 844 tall, fold drawn at 843. The header is 68px, which is 10.4px less than the web one for two reasons that pull opposite ways: the title is --text-lg rather than --text-2xl, and the padding is --space-4 rather than --space-5. The add control is the same 36px box with a 44px target around it, so it looks identical on both platforms and only its target differs. The content area is 788px, which is 844 less the 56px header and not the 610px the ticket quotes: 610 is the detail screen's, measured between a 136px playlist header and a 98px bar, and this screen has the first as its items rather than above them. The header is 56px because the add control is a floating button, so the header carries only the title.

Each item is the header's own card in a two-column grid: a square cover, the play and the shuffle on the picture, the identity below it.

Two columns put six whole cards above the fold, and four with the bar docked. The card is 172.2px wide with a 140.2px square cover and 223.6px tall, and it needs no second cover size, because .mart is aspect-ratio: 1 and square by construction. Three is not available and that is measured rather than preferred: at 111.33px the card's content needs 154px, the action row is 76px against a 47.33px cover content box, and the 80px control band would be 100.9% of a 79.33px cover, taller than the picture it sits on.

Six things are dropped or moved here, and all six are this screen's decisions rather than the component's. The overflow is gone, because renaming, deleting, managing shares and syncing on demand are all available from inside the playlist on a phone. The sync line does not get a line of its own: the state joins the count, at opposite ends of it, and the time is left off. The name drops a step from --text-xl to --text-lg, 24px to 20px. Both rows of the card end on the same edge, so the share indicator sits at the right of the name rather than beside it. And two controls are gone from the picture: there is no transport, and the shuffle has moved to the top left corner.

The band's one control is the same control in both states. The header's own play, becoming the header's own pause when this is the playlist that is playing, and nothing moves when it starts: the band keeps its --space-4 padding and the play stays where it was. The cost is named rather than absorbed, because a card at 172.2px has nowhere to put the equalizer strip that says what is playing on web and no room for two steps, so the pause glyph is the entire signal and it is 48px of button on a gallery of six.

The add control is a floating action button here and a labelled button in the header on web. A 56px circle, inset --space-4 from the bottom right, sitting 113px up when the 97px bar is docked so it clears the bar by 16px. It covers 32.5% of a card's width. There is no shadow token in this system, so it brings its own ground the way the shuffle chip does, and it is --accent on --bg, the 5.85:1 the button's legend already had.

It has no visible word, and the glyph's size was re-decided because of that. The name is on aria-label; the glyph is a plus, which is the one mark that needs no learning. The header's button put the plus at --icon-sm beside a --text-sm label, a pair; with no label there is no pair, and 16px in a 56px circle is 29% of the box against --icon-lg's 50%. The extended pill carrying "Add" was 98 by 56px, 56.9% of a card's width, and was rejected for it.

What the button costs is scroll. It does not scroll with the grid, so it needs clearance below the last card or it hides that card permanently: 88px at rest and 185px with the bar docked, 23.8% of the content area for a control occupying 15.7% of the width. At the resting fold it covers no card content at all, measured against every card's picture, name and status line; it sits over the last card's own background.

It sits at --z-sticky, which was first written as --z-overlay and corrected by drawing it under the dialog it opens. Three requirements meet there: above the grid, below the bar at --z-player, and below the scrim at --z-overlay. At the scrim's own tier it sits in front of the scrim dimming it: measured, three points on the button hit the scrim at --z-sticky and the button itself at --z-overlay.

The shuffle needs its own ground, and it cannot have a scrim. The band's is 84px of opaque --surface-raised with the falloff reaching 108px, so a second band at the top would be 192px of a 140.2px picture. A 32px chip is 5.2% of it instead, and its contrast is computed rather than depended on what is behind it, which is the header's own reason for the scrim's last stop being opaque rather than 60% black.

It is inset 24px, so the chip and the play share a centre rather than a left edge: they are a 32px box and a 48px box, and matching their left edges puts their centres 8px apart, which reads as misaligned because the eye lines up centres and not edges. Measured, both centres are 40.8px from the picture's on every card, resting and playing, and the 44px and 48px targets are stacked on one axis. It was on the right and inset 4.2px, derived from the transport's right edge being 4.2px from the picture's so that the shuffle and the track-next icon shared an x. That derivation had nothing left to point at once the transport was gone.

The count is at the left, the state at the right, and there is no separator. The words are 57px, 70.8px and 73.76px wide and only right alignment makes the longest of them read as a column edge, so a Sync failed sits at the same x on every card. A middot between two things at opposite ends would be a third thing floating in the middle, so the --space-2 gap is the separator, and it is a floor rather than a figure: measured, it runs from 8px on a full line to 52.62px on the emptiest.

Keeping both the noun and the dot is paid for with the card's height. At opposite ends "1284 tracks" is 74.66px and Sync failed with its dot is 73.76px, so 148.42px of a 140.2px column: over by 8.22, and dropping the count's number to --text-xs still needs 143.64px, over by 3.44. So the state holds its size and the count yields, and what drops to a line of its own is the noun and never the figure. Measured on the six fixtures: four cards at 223.6px and two at 247.3px, so the grid no longer has a constant card height, which pairing the two facts had bought.

The line is aligned on baseline rather than centred, because the number is --text-sm and the word is --text-xs, and centring a 14px figure beside a 12px word puts two baselines in one sentence: measured, align-items: center puts them 1.6px apart. Measured on every card carrying both facts, the number and the word are each 14.4px from the top of the line, 0px apart. It works because of a coincidence worth naming: a flex item with no baseline has one synthesized from its border box, and .state's first item is the dot, so the baseline flexbox picks is the dot's bottom edge, which measures 11.6px inside a 16.2px state. That is the same 11.6px the word's own baseline is at, which is why no correction is needed. Change the dot's height and the word lifts by the difference, and nothing in the tree checks it.

The price of the grid is the name, and the step buys back two characters of it. The name's column is 112px, of which 28px is the share indicator, so about 84px of text: some ten characters at --text-lg against eight at --text-xl. Two of the 27 that fitted at one card per row. What pays for the rest of the card's height is the status line, not the name: the card was 329px to 345.2px with the header's full sync line under the name, 247.3px with the state and the count on two lines, and 223.6px with them on one. The name row is 140.2px, of which the share indicator takes 20px and the row's own gap 8px, leaving 112.2px of text, and four of the six fixture names are cut.

The scrim stays and still covers 57.1% of a 140.2px cover, against 24.7% of the 324.4px cover one card per row gave it. That is the cost of leading with the artwork in a grid and it contradicts the header's own argument that a band has to be short enough not to eat the artwork. It is recorded as a cost rather than answered, because the grid is worth more than the band.

The three overflows, one per access level

On web only. A mobile card carries no overflow at all: renaming, deleting, managing shares and syncing on demand are all available from inside the playlist on a phone, so a card has no menu and no button for one. The access gating is a web question now, and this section is where it is answered.

access is on PlaylistSchema as owner or a ShareLevel, and the glossary is explicit about what a collaborator may do: add a track, remove a track, sync, and not rename, delete or manage shares, those three staying with the owner. So the header's four items fall into two groups and a collaborator's overflow holds one of them.

A read collaborator gets no overflow at all, rather than one with nothing in it, because there is no action to take from this screen. The header's own no-tracks case argues the opposite about Delete, that the overflow is exactly when you want it, and that is true there and does not apply here: Delete is the owner's.

The two collaborator items carry the same glyph, which is the honest picture of the permission model: the indicator is coarser than access is, one mark covering both collaborator levels, so what a person may do to an item is invisible until its menu is open and then it is exact.

One item per arrangement, because the menu opens downward and is 170px tall against a 137px item, so two open at once overlap. In the product opening one closes the others, so that is the specimen and not the component.

The owner: all four, with the rule before the one that destroys it. That rule is the header's, and the reason is that rename and delete four pixels apart with nothing between them is a mis-click waiting to happen.

The empty gallery

No playlists at all, which is a different component from the playlist empty state and not a variant of it. That one is a playlist that has never been synced and its action is Sync now; this one's action is Add a playlist, and the two actions are the whole of the difference. Drawing the other would tell a person with no playlists at all to sync something.

The sixth row in the web list is a probe and not a fixture. Its name is 68 characters and measures 896px, which is the whole of the name column on a row carrying an indicator, so it fits exactly and does not ellipsise. That is the finding rather than an accident: at 1056 the web arrangement has no truncation case at all, and a name long enough to produce one would have to be about 924px wide at --text-md. The word Add a playlist appears twice on the empty screen, once in the header and once on the gallery, and that is a duplication rather than an oversight. The two never coexist with a list, so at no width does a user see it twice while choosing between two things.

The bar arriving

There is no bar at rest, and this is the state that replaced that one. The bar is 98px as the player bar record measures it, less one pixel: the docked bar is 97px, because its bottom border is against the screen edge where a border draws nothing. The arrangement is that record's flush one, since this is a 390 screen, and its shuffle is in the markup and hidden by rule rather than left out, which is the difference between a control that is not drawn and one that does not exist. What the arrival costs is named rather than argued: the content area drops from 775px to 678px and from 8 whole rows to 7, so the arriving bar takes a row the user could see, on the one screen where they are about to tap one.

Signed out, at 390

Mobile only, and it replaces the content area rather than sitting over it: signed out, no other resource in the app is reachable, so there is no list behind it to dim. Web has no signed-out state at all, and that is not an omission, because the edge authenticates the browser before the app exists. There is no control here and the sentence says so, which is the whole design of the state.

What the item is, in one paragraph

A playlist item is the playlist header, at its own 136px on web and as its own square card on mobile. This screen is a gallery of headers and adds four things to one: the links on its cover and its name, the list it stacks in, the permission facts its overflow is gated on, and the door it is instead of a play, since a playlist is started from its own screen and the item's whole surface opens it. Everything else, including the rules, belongs to that record and to specimen.css. An earlier version of this screen drew an 88.8px row carrying the header's identity block alone, with its own cover, name line, count and indicator under five class names of its own, and it had already reproduced flex: 1 on the name, which is the mistake the header's own comment says that line made first.

What is not drawn

The owner of a shared playlist, by name, is now drawn on the two collaborator items above as Shared by Ada and Shared by Grace, resolved from ownerAccountId through GET /accounts. An item whose owner is not on the directory page keeps the indicator alone rather than a placeholder, because a placeholder is read as a decision.

An equalizer strip on a card, which its own size cannot carry: a square cover at 324px gives 51.4 bars against the strip's 7n - 4 and its reach of 178px is behind the 80px of controls it would have to cross. Nor is one drawn on a library item, which carries no play: the strip is the detail header's, where the playlist it belongs to is the one being played.

The queue panel, which belongs to the player bar record rather than to a screen. The library is one of the places it can be opened from and not a place it is designed.

A search field, a sort control, a filter, or any pagination. Search is a permanent non-goal and the page size is PageQuerySchema's default of 50, which is the API's business and not a control this screen draws. The consequence is that a gallery of more than 50 playlists is a gallery this screen cannot show all of, and that is recorded rather than solved by inventing a control the glossary has ruled out.

One thing an earlier version of this section listed here is now drawn, the sync state, and saying so is the point of the list. It was recorded as a deliberate absence with a reason, and it is present because an item is the header rather than a smaller component. The strip is the other half of the same fact and is not drawn here: a gallery item carries no play, so the strip belongs to the detail header, where the playlist it belongs to is the one being played.

Usage

Do keep the list one list. A playlist shared to the account is in it, and a second section for shared items would duplicate the row and split an empty gallery into two meanings. Do not restate the count as something playable: the count is a total of associations, so a playlist of 300 with 290 still pending reads 300, and nothing on a row may claim otherwise. Do not add a playlist count to the header for the same reason a figure is not a colour here: a standing total spends attention that belongs to the list.
