PlayLiberty design / icons
Foundations

Icons

19 glyphs, all of them Tabler's, drawn on its 24 unit grid with a 2 unit stroke and coloured only by whatever they sit inside. The size scale on this page was measured in the controls the glyphs go in, because a glyph does not fill its box and the grid cannot be read off the drawing. Every number in the last section came out of the geometry.

The set

One filled glyph, the rest outline. The role is what a glyph is for here, and the name beside it is the one to import.

player-play-filled the primary play button
player-pause pause, on that same button
player-skip-back previous track, in the player bar
player-track-next next track, in the player bar
arrows-shuffle shuffle, on the header and the player bar
repeat repeat, in the player bar
dots-vertical more actions, on the header and on every track row
trash delete a playlist, in the menu
check a checked box
minus a partially selected box
refresh a sync in progress
alert-triangle a sync that failed
circle-x a track that can never be played
clock never synced, and how long ago otherwise
playlist the empty library
pencil rename a playlist, in the menu
rotate-clockwise sync now from the menu, and retry on a failed track
share share a playlist, in the menu
cloud-download the one action in the empty state

The size scale

Three steps, each named for the job. 20 is 0.62 of the 32px control and 28 is 0.58 of the 48px one, so the two sit at a single ratio of about 0.6. 16 is the exception and is 0.89 of the checkbox, because a mark inside a control fills the control rather than sitting in it.

--icon-lg, 28px, in the 48px primary
--icon-md, 20px, in the 32px control
--icon-sm, 16px, in the 18px box

The play triangle is nudged 1.5 units left of where the box puts it. Tabler centres that glyph on its optical bounds rather than on its box, which is correct for a glyph on its own and wrong for a glyph in a round button, where 1.5 units is 1.75px at this size and reads as a triangle sitting off to the right. Of the 19, 6 are off centre and 4 of those are moved; check and pencil are off by less than half a unit and are left alone, because a correction that small changes nothing on screen. This is a position and not a size: the glyph is still drawn at the token size, so it does not contradict the rule that both clients agree on one number per step.

The width column runs from 0.17 on the three dots to 0.93 on the cloud, which is a factor of five and a half. A scale read off the grid would have put all of these at one optical size, and they are at four. The two ends of the range are not both mistakes: a mark that is 0.08 of its box in height is a single stroke and is supposed to look thin, and a glyph at 0.93 is nearly filling its box and will crowd a control the others sit comfortably inside.

The ones that are not like the others

10 of the 19, and what each one costs or needs because of it.

player-play-filled The one filled glyph, and the one that sits 1.5 units right of its own centre.
player-pause Outline, because a filled pause would mean nothing an outline does not.
arrows-shuffle Tabler calls it arrows-shuffle. There is no icon named shuffle at this pin.
dots-vertical Three dots and their caps, so 0.17 of the width is the whole mark, which makes it the faintest thing in the set. At --icon-sm in a 28px row control the three dots span 2.7px and each is 1.3px across, narrower than the character it replaced. It is legible and it is the one mark here whose weight is set by its glyph rather than by its box.
trash Separated by a rule and coloured as a fault, because it is the destructive one, and it takes that colour from the row rather than carrying one.
check Off by half a unit and deliberately not corrected: 0.33px inside an 18px box is below where a correction would show.
minus One stroke, so 0.08 of the height is the whole mark.
pencil Off by a third of a unit and deliberately not corrected: 0.21px at --icon-sm, which is below where a correction would show.
rotate-clockwise The action, so it is the glyph a user presses to sync. Deliberately not the release's own refresh glyph, which plays the state a sync leaves behind: an action cannot share a glyph with the state it produces, and a reader looking for the product's word in the icon set finds two candidates and this is the one that is pressable.
cloud-download The arrow hangs below the cloud, so the mark sits low in its box.

What is not an icon

Drawn by this system, on purpose, and worth saying out loud.

the checkbox square Control geometry. The box is the control, and a glyph drawn inside it would be a second set of measurements for a shape the browser already has a box for.
the radio circle and its dot Control geometry, for the same reason.
the switch track and knob Control geometry. Tabler has switch icons, and using one would mean the control's own state was drawn twice at two sizes.
the segmented control's rule Control geometry, and it is the selection indicator rather than a glyph, which is why it carries no icon at all.
the checkbox tick and the partial bar Icons, and they are the two glyphs that cannot be markup. A native input holds no children, so they arrive as a mask with the pinned path inside it, painted in currentColor so the mark takes the colour of the control it is in.
