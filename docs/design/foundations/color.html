Colour

Instrument black, one deep green telltale, one amber telltale used sparingly, and album art as the only large field of colour. Every value is a named object in that scene, and every ratio on this page was computed from the two values rather than judged by eye.

The second colour, and what it is for

Amber. Not a surface, not a state and not the action colour, so it has a job of its own rather than borrowing one of theirs.

--emphasis #e39a3c 7.66:1 A telltale lit to warn. It is the brightest thing the system puts on screen after the album art, and it earns that by being rare.
the rule and the mark A notice carries amber on its edge and its mark and keeps its sentence in ink. Tinting the sentence as well is the mistake: once the words are amber they stop being the thing being pointed at.
3 a filled badge 8.30:1 Near-black on amber, the same inversion the green button uses. A count that wants looking at.

Three rules, and they are what keep a second colour from becoming noise. One emphasis per view, or the emphasis is the background. Always with a word, never the colour alone, which is the same rule the six tones obey. And never on a hover, which is what the primary button used to do: a statement that fires every time a pointer crosses a control is not a statement.

--tone-downloading holds this same literal, and the suite fails if the two ever stop agreeing. They cannot be one token pointing at the other, because the design record rejects aliased tokens: React Native cannot resolve var(). “In progress” is a mild form of “needs attention”, so a state sharing a colour's name is ordinary; a colour borrowing a state name is not, and that is what this token used to be.

They can share a value because they never compete. Download concurrency is capped at 2, so at most two rows are ever downloading at once and neither of them is the emphasis. That cap is load-bearing for this decision: at 20, a screen full of downloading rows would leave the emphasis with nothing to stand out from, and it would need a value of its own.

Six tones, one per state

Every state carries a label, so colour never works alone. Ratios are against --surface-raised, the hardest surface in the system.
