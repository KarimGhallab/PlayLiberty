## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment: the state model is authored, the shell is not, and the typeface that would carry the authored part never loads.**

What could only be PlayLiberty: "Shared by Dev User" in its own sky beside a lock mark; the amber rule under a playing playlist; "Up to date · Synced yesterday" as a sentence rather than a badge; the never-synced copy that states the retention promise word for word; the egress console in a distinct operator role; a current row found by accent, word and bars rather than by colour alone; a removed source that keeps a word ("Stored") instead of silently vanishing.

What any media app could ship unchanged: the dark ground, the floating pill player, the bottom sheets, the accent FAB, the two-column cover grid. That is the default kit of the category, and the app does not push against it.

Two findings turn this from a taste argument into a defect:

1. **The typeface is not delivered.** `packages/tokens/src/values.ts` names `IBM Plex Sans`, `apps/mobile/src/shared/styles/tokens.ts` passes it through, and the app has no font asset, no `expo-font` dependency, no plugin and no loader in `app/_layout.tsx`. Android resolves the unknown family to the platform default, so every screen renders in Roboto. The type scale, the weights and the 1.3-capped layouts were tuned against Plex metrics and are running against different ones.
2. **The product's own hero state is invisible.** The glossary calls remote-removed ("stored" plus source "removed") "the promise of the product". It has no count, no chip and no group. The only aggregate about removals is "Unavailable 4" in the failure tone, and the only rows it reveals are four identical `[Deleted video]` rows with no art, `--:--` and no action. A user who came to be reassured that removals cannot cost them a track is shown only the removals that were not saved.

## Overall Impression

A quiet, carefully built player whose craftsmanship lives in details nobody will name (the seek bar, the undo window, the in-place revoke confirm, contrast that passes everywhere at 4.60:1 worst case) and whose weakest point is the one action the product is built around. The single biggest opportunity is not a redesign: it is to make sync reachable and to give the retention promise a surface, both of which the design doc already specifies.

## What's Working

1. **The bar and the queue are one object.** The player bar appears only with a first track, persists across every route, publishes its own height so pages pay it as padding, shows queue position, and the playing playlist carries the amber rule. The user never wonders what is playing or where it lives.
2. **The copy at anxious moments is product-specific and calm.** `PlaylistEmpty` and `SyncBand` say what is kept, what is gone and what happens next, in the user's words, with the server's own reason attached on failure. This is the strongest expression of the brand the app has.
3. **The accessibility floor is genuinely built, not claimed.** 73 interactive elements, 52 accessible labels, 70 of 73 with an explicit non-default role, zero hardcoded English labels, complete catalog parity between English and French (412 leaf keys, empty difference both ways), every contrast pair computed and passing, and reduced motion honoured by 15 of 19 animation paths.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Sync, the recurring verb, has no control on the screen that needs it

**What:** `PlaylistIdentity.tsx` renders the state line as text only ("Up to date · yesterday"). Sync exists only inside the actions sheet (dots, then Sync) and inside the library's floating dial. `docs/DESIGN.md` states the phone trims sync to a named control beside the state line; that control is not implemented.

**Why it matters:** A playlist only keeps its promise if it is synced, and the household member will never learn a two-level menu. The one action that makes the product work is the least reachable action on every surface.

**Fix:** Add the bordered sync control to the meta row in `PlaylistIdentity.tsx`, disabled and busy-state aware while a run is live, keeping the sheet row as the secondary path.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] The retention promise has no surface, and the status words contradict each other

**What:** `PlaylistCard.tsx` derives "Up to date" from `syncStatus` and separately draws a download glyph with a count in the failure tone, with no word, and `cardLabel` omits the count entirely. The detail's chip says "Unavailable" while the row says "Removed from YouTube". Nothing aggregates remote-removed tracks, which `docs/GLOSSARY.md` calls the promise of the product.

**Why it matters:** A user cannot tell whether the red 4 is downloading, failed or permanently lost, cannot verify the one thing they came for, and hears the count twice as silence from a screen reader. The app raises alarm about removals it could not save and stays silent about the ones it did save, which is the inverse of the reassurance this product sells.

**Fix:** Give the count a word and put it in `cardLabel`; add a remote-removed count or word to `statusChips.ts` and the header; use one term across chip and row. `statusChips.ts` is already the single owner for a state's chip word, so extend it rather than adding a second vocabulary.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] A gallery card is four focus stops, and every row is two

**What:** The Android accessibility tree exposes each card as four independent stops (the card, `playlist-play`, `playlist-shuffle`, `playlist-actions-*`), and every track row adds a 48dp lane stop. A 71-track playlist is 142 stops. `docs/DESIGN.md` states a gallery card is one target whose name carries the count, the sync state and the owner; the name is complete except the count.

**Why it matters:** For a screen-reader user, browsing the library is a linear march through buttons before reaching content, and the card's own name does not say what the red 4 means. This is the difference between an app that works with TalkBack and one that is tiring with it.

**Fix:** Collapse the card to one accessible element with the count in its label and expose play, shuffle and actions through the card's own actions or a single trailing control; keep the row's lane but drop the duplicate play layer from the tree.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit`

### [P2] The typeface never loads

**What:** No font asset, no `expo-font` dependency, no plugin, no loader. `fontFamily: 'IBM Plex Sans'` resolves to nothing and every screen falls back to Roboto.

**Why it matters:** The entire typographic system, including the capped 1.3 layouts that were measured against Plex, is an approximation. It is also the single cheapest reason the app reads as stock Android rather than as itself.

**Fix:** Bundle Plex Sans (three weights, one variable file as the web does) and load it in `app/_layout.tsx` or through the Expo font plugin, gating the first frame; keep `families['font-ui']` as the only owner of the name.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable typeset`

### [P2] Unintended actions fire without a guard

**What:** `AccountSheet.tsx` signs out on one press of a red row at the sheet's bottom edge, where a thumb rests, and the cost is a slow browser re-provision. A horizontal swipe across a track row starts playback; it was triggered by accident during this review. Three `Animated.spring` settle-backs (`PlayerBar` swipe, two in `Notice`) and the detail header's per-frame lerp ignore `useReduceMotion`.

**Why it matters:** The two places a thumb naturally lands are the two places that act without asking. Reduced motion is a stated product commitment and four paths still animate under it.

**Fix:** Reuse the in-place confirm the Share sheet already uses for revoke; require a deliberate vertical intent or a longer threshold before a row swipe plays; guard the three springs and make the header snap under reduced motion.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

## Persona Red Flags

**Casey (one-handed, interrupted, 411dp wide).** What a thumb can reach (the bar, the FAB) works. Everything else sits at the top: the scope and status rail at about 74dp, the account and egress doors, the detail's dots and chevron. Sync is behind those dots. The detail header is a drag-dismiss handle at every offset, so a scroll that starts high on a 230dp header can dismiss the page. At rest the active filter chip is clipped, so she cannot read which filter is applied. Sign out is one tap at the sheet's bottom edge.

**Sam (TalkBack, low vision, 200% text).** The card is four stops and the row is two, so the library and a long playlist are a march of buttons. The red count of 4 is silent. The egress card's facts are unlabelled text pairs, so "Status" and "OK" arrive as separate utterances. At 1.3 text scale the selected status chip leaves the screen while staying focusable, and titles truncate to about 15 characters. The caps themselves hold: nothing overflows the header or the cards. Contrast is the one thing Sam does not have to fight: worst case is 4.60:1 for text and 3.03:1 for a boundary.

**Alex (impatient power user).** Sync is two taps and never visible. Sort and filter scroll away after two rows, with no Android equivalent of tapping the status bar to return to the top. No jump to the current row, no queue editing beyond the list, no search (a stated non-goal, but at 1,275 tracks it is noticed). What works for Alex: bulk sync, adding one video to several playlists at once, instant language switch, swipe-to-skip, and the bar's artwork opening its playlist.

**The household member who needs a removed video not to cost them a track.** They open the library and see "Unavailable 9" in the failure tone, a red download count of 4 on "Chine", and "Up to date" beside it. They filter to Unavailable and get four identical `[Deleted video]` rows with no art, `--:--`, "Removed from YouTube" and no action. Nothing anywhere says "9 tracks had their video removed and all 9 still play", because that state has no surface. Their other risk is a stray tap on Sign out, which costs a browser re-provision.

## Questions to Consider

1. Which promise is the library selling: the one it can keep (a stored copy survives) or the one it cannot (the source is gone)? Today it foregrounds the second.
2. Why is the one recurring verb, sync, the least reachable action on every surface?
3. If the accent means "this is the action", why does the library's only accent control open a menu?
4. At 11 playlists and 1,275 tracks with no search and a two-column grid, at what size does this gallery stop being navigable?
5. Why does the app translate its own name? A brand that changes spelling with the interface language gives up the one word it owns.
